Why shrinkage worked but didn't cure

The bucket-WR estimator has near-zero IC after price-controlling (Sprint 13b theoretical finding). Bayesian shrinkage correctly pulls small-sample WRs toward the market prior, reducing the magnitude of phantom edges and the frequency of Kelly entries. But when the underlying signal has no true predictive content, even a mathematically optimal estimator produces near-random trades. The residual −$221 loss is information-theoretic, not sizing-theoretic — it is the cost of Kelly operating on a zero-IC estimator at all, regardless of how well the variance is controlled.

The k = 20 + 3√N derivation

k_base = 20 is the analytical minimum (not a tuned heuristic):
shrunk_edge(W=2, N=5, p=0.20, k) = (W - N·p) / (N + k) = 1/(5+k) Half-Kelly safety cap requires edge ≤ 0.04: 1/(5+k) ≤ 0.04 ⇒ k ≥ 20

This is the smallest pseudocount that guarantees a 1σ noise event at N=5 cannot breach the 5% per-trade Kelly cap. Anything less would allow the 15–24¢ steamroller to reappear.

α·√N floor is Empirical Bayes by construction:

The relative prior weight k/(N+k) decays at rate 1/√N, which is isomorphic to the standard error of a proportion (σ_p = √(p(1−p)/N)). Prior authority fades in lockstep with the data's statistical precision.

Decision 3: The Two-Gate Stack as the activation policy

Gate A (Information): ML model must produce Brier < 0.05 AND Jaccard ≥ 0.70 across CV folds.
Gate B (Variance control): Shrinkage wraps ML edge with k = 20 + 3√N pseudocount before it reaches Kelly.

Both gates required. Information without variance control reproduces Phase C naive (−$477/55% DD). Variance control without information reproduces Phase C shrunk (−$221/34% DD — still negative EV on a zero-IC signal).
